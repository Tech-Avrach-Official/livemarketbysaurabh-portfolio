Four directions, Indian trading styled

Direction B is the gold one from the last round, kept unchanged as the reference. C, D and E are new, each built around a different reading of what an Indian trading brand looks like — street energy, broadcast authority, and festive premium.

All three new directions are built for an ambient hero video — a muted loop playing full-bleed behind the headline, with an unmute control. The bull footage shown is a still standing in for that loop.

Every direction keeps light and dark mode. Switch themes from the toolbar above.

Direction B

Premium Gold

The one you liked, unchanged. Gold-dominant palette, gradient headline, bullion motif, glowing rounded CTA, large price cards. Kept here as the reference the new directions are measured against.

Tradeoff. Closest to the visual language of paid signals channels. Also the only direction here whose hero is still a portrait rather than video — it takes the same ambient-loop treatment if you want it.

Direction C

Bazaar — desi bold

What Indian trading channels on Telegram and YouTube actually look like. Full-bleed ambient video behind a heavy condensed headline, saffron-to-marigold heat gradient, Devanagari line carrying the real message, prices set loud and bold.

After close

Review. Trades posted and picked apart — what worked, what didn't, and why. Losses included.

On the video. The hero is designed for a muted 6–10s loop, no controls, with an unmute affordance. Keep it under ~2MB, preload="none", poster frame set, and pause it off-screen — on a page that already spends its budget on chart iframes, an uncompressed hero video is the single easiest way to lose the under-2.5s target.

On the numbers. Prices are illustrative sample values in the existing “indicative / 15-min delayed” framing. Every other fact — 3,500+ members, 8:30 pre-market, free, no tips — comes from the live site. No performance or returns figures were invented.

On the Hindi. The Devanagari lines in C and E are mine, not Saurabh’s — get him to rewrite them in his own words before anything ships. A borrowed voice in the mother tongue is more obvious than a borrowed voice in English.
