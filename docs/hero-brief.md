## 1. The photo — the important one

### What to shoot

**Saurabh at his actual desk, working.** Screens on, charts up, mid-thought.
Not posed for the camera.

The photo already in `public/logo.png` is a studio-style portrait in a
blazer, arms to camera. As a 28px mark in the header it is perfect. As a
hero image it would push the page toward guru territory — which is exactly
what the other ten sections are built to avoid. So this is a new shot.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| At the desk, screens visible behind or beside | Plain wall or studio backdrop |
| Looking at the screens, or mid-conversation | Staring down the camera, arms crossed |
| Normal clothes — shirt, kurta, whatever he wears | Blazer and tie |
| One side light, window light is ideal | Direct flash, or a ring light |
| A little mess — mug, notebook, cables | Staged, empty, spotless desk |

### Three practical things that matter more than they sound

**Turn off portrait mode.** Phone camera is fine, but fake background blur
falls apart when the image is shown large. Real depth from a real lens, or
nothing.

**Mid-tone background.** The site has a light theme and a dark theme now. A
photo shot against a very dark or very bright background will clash with
one of them. A normal room at normal brightness sits comfortably in both.

**No readable account screens.** Charts are good. Live P&L, account balance,
order book with real positions — crop them out or blur them before sending.
That is a privacy and compliance issue, not an aesthetic one.

### Specs

- **Portrait orientation, 4:5** (e.g. 1600×2000). It sits beside the
  headline, so vertical.
- **Minimum 1600px on the short side.** Bigger is fine, I will resize.
- **Leave space around him** — don't fill the frame with his face. I need
  room to crop for mobile, where it sits above the text rather than beside.
- **Shoot 3–4 frames**, varying distance: one wide with the desk, one waist
  up, one head-and-shoulders. I will test which reads best at hero size.
- Send originals, not WhatsApp-compressed copies.
